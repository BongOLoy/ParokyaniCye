Manuel Luis Quezon y Molina

PRESIDENT • 1935 – 1944

BIOGRAPHY

Early Life and Education

Manuel Luis Quezon y Molina was born on August 19, 1878, in Baler, Tayabas, which is now part of Aurora Province. His parents were Lucio Quezon and Maria Dolores Molina. He grew up during the Spanish colonial period, a time when Filipinos were beginning to demand greater rights and freedom from foreign rule.

Quezon studied at Colegio de San Juan de Letran in Manila and later attended the University of Santo Tomas, where he studied law. His education helped prepare him for a career in public service. During the Philippine Revolution, he also served with the forces of Emilio Aguinaldo before continuing his studies and entering government work.

Political Career

After passing the bar examination in 1903, Quezon began working in government as a lawyer and provincial fiscal. He later became governor of Tayabas and was elected to the First Philippine Assembly in 1907. His ability as a speaker and political leader helped him gain recognition in national affairs.

In 1909, he became the Philippine Resident Commissioner in the United States, where he worked to represent Filipino interests. He later returned to the Philippines and became a senator in 1916. He served as Senate President and continued leading efforts to gain greater self-government and eventual independence for the country.

Presidency and Achievements

In 1935, Manuel L. Quezon was elected the first President of the Commonwealth of the Philippines. His administration focused on preparing the country for independence, strengthening the government, improving public welfare, and building a stronger national identity.

One of his important contributions was supporting the development of a national language. In 1937, the government selected Tagalog as the basis of the national language, which later developed into Filipino. Quezon also promoted social justice, labor protection, and programs intended to improve the lives of ordinary Filipinos.

His government also worked on national defense and preparations for self-government. The Commonwealth was designed as a transition period before the Philippines would become fully independent. Quezon played an important role in guiding the country through this period.

World War II and Later Years

During World War II, Japanese forces invaded the Philippines in 1941. Quezon and other members of the Commonwealth government eventually left the country and established a government-in-exile in the United States. Even while away from the Philippines, he continued to represent the Commonwealth and support the country's cause.

Quezon's health became weaker during his time in the United States. He died on August 1, 1944, in Saranac Lake, New York, before the Philippines was liberated from Japanese occupation. He was later honored through the naming of Quezon City, which became an important center of government and national life.

Manuel L. Quezon is remembered for his leadership during the Commonwealth period, his efforts to advance Philippine independence, and his support for a national language. His work helped shape the government and identity of the modern Philippine nation.
