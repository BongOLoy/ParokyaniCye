Benigno Simeon Cojuangco Aquino III

PRESIDENT • 2010 – 2016

BIOGRAPHY

Early Life and Family Background

Benigno Simeon Cojuangco Aquino III was born on February 8, 1960, in Manila, Philippines. He was the only son and third child of former Senator Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr. and Corazon C. Aquino, who later became the eleventh President of the Philippines.

He grew up in a family deeply involved in Philippine politics. His father was a well-known opposition leader during the administration of Ferdinand Marcos Sr. After his father's assassination in 1983, his mother became a leading figure in the movement that opposed the Marcos government and eventually became president following the 1986 People Power Revolution.

Aquino studied at Ateneo de Manila University, where he earned a degree in Economics in 1981. His education helped develop his understanding of economic and public issues, which later became important in his political career.

Early Career and Entry into Politics

Before entering government, Aquino worked in the private sector and became involved in business activities. He also supported his family during the political struggles of the 1980s.

In 1998, he entered politics when he was elected as the representative of the Second District of Tarlac in the House of Representatives. He served three terms in the House and participated in legislative work involving government accountability and public welfare.

In 2007, Aquino was elected to the Philippine Senate. As a senator, he supported measures related to transparency, public accountability, and government reform. His political popularity increased following the death of his mother in 2009.

Election and Presidency

Aquino ran for president in the 2010 national elections under the Liberal Party. His campaign emphasized honesty, transparency, and fighting corruption in government. He won the election and became the fifteenth President of the Philippines on June 30, 2010.

His administration adopted the slogan "Daang Matuwid," which means "Straight Path." It represented his promise to promote good governance, reduce corruption, and improve the delivery of public services.

Major Programs and Achievements

One of the important developments during Aquino's presidency was the implementation of the K to 12 education reform. The program added two years to basic education, introducing Senior High School to help students prepare for higher education, employment, and other opportunities.

His administration also supported the Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive Health Act, which became law in 2012. The law aimed to improve access to reproductive health information, services, and family-planning education.

Another major measure was the Sin Tax Reform Act of 2012. It increased taxes on tobacco and alcohol products, with part of the additional revenue intended to support public health programs and expand access to healthcare.

Aquino's administration also continued the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program, or 4Ps, which provided conditional cash assistance to qualified low-income families. The program encouraged school attendance and regular health checkups for children and their families.

Economic Growth and Infrastructure

During his presidency, the Philippines experienced periods of strong economic growth. His administration promoted investment, improved public financial management, and pursued reforms intended to strengthen the country's economy.

The government also expanded infrastructure projects through the Public-Private Partnership program. This approach encouraged cooperation between the government and private companies in developing transportation, public facilities, and other important services.

Foreign Relations and the West Philippine Sea

Aquino's administration placed strong attention on the country's territorial rights in the West Philippine Sea. In 2013, the Philippine government initiated an arbitration case against China under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.

In July 2016, an international arbitral tribunal ruled in favor of the Philippines on several important legal issues concerning maritime rights. The decision became a significant development in the country's efforts to defend its rights under international law.

Challenges During His Administration

Despite economic progress and government reforms, Aquino faced criticism over several issues. His administration received public criticism for its response to Typhoon Yolanda in 2013, which caused widespread destruction and affected millions of Filipinos.

Another major controversy involved the Mamasapano incident in January 2015, where 44 members of the Philippine National Police Special Action Force were killed during an operation in Maguindanao. The incident raised questions about the planning and leadership of the operation.

His administration also faced criticism over problems involving public transportation, government services, and the distribution of economic benefits. These issues affected public opinion during the later years of his presidency.

End of Presidency and Later Life

Aquino completed his six-year term on June 30, 2016. He was succeeded by Rodrigo Roa Duterte. After leaving office, he returned to private life and remained involved in discussions about Philippine politics and national issues.

Benigno Aquino III died on June 24, 2021, in Quezon City, at the age of 61. His death was mourned by many Filipinos, and he was remembered for his years of public service and his role in modern Philippine history.

Legacy

Benigno Aquino III is remembered for his administration's focus on transparency, economic growth, education reform, and public accountability. His presidency also included important developments in Philippine foreign policy, especially concerning the West Philippine Sea.

His time in office had both achievements and challenges. His administration remains an important part of the country's recent history because of its reforms, major national events, and efforts to strengthen democratic institutions.

Public service is measured by the responsibility, honesty, and dedication shown to the people.
