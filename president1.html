Emilio Aguinaldo y Famy

PRESIDENT • 1899 – 1901

BIOGRAPHY

Early Life

Emilio Aguinaldo y Famy was born on March 22, 1869, in Kawit, Cavite. He grew up in a family that was involved in local community affairs. During his younger years, he studied in Manila, including at Colegio de San Juan de Letran. However, he did not finish his studies and later returned to Cavite.

In 1895, Aguinaldo became the municipal captain of Kawit. During the same year, he joined the Katipunan, a secret organization that aimed to free the Philippines from Spanish rule. He became one of the important leaders of the revolutionary movement in Cavite.

Role in the Philippine Revolution

When the Philippine Revolution began in 1896, Aguinaldo led Filipino forces in Cavite and gained recognition through several victories against Spanish troops. He later became a leading figure in the revolutionary government after the Tejeros Convention in March 1897.

Due to continued fighting against Spain, Aguinaldo and other revolutionary leaders entered into the Pact of Biak-na-Bato in December 1897. The agreement led to his exile in Hong Kong. However, he returned to the Philippines on May 19, 1898, to continue the struggle for independence.

Declaration of Independence and Presidency

One of the most important moments in Aguinaldo's life happened on June 12, 1898. On this date, he proclaimed Philippine independence from Spain at his home in Kawit, Cavite. The Philippine flag was officially unfurled, and the national anthem was played during the ceremony.

On January 23, 1899, the First Philippine Republic was inaugurated in Malolos, Bulacan, and Aguinaldo became its first president. His government faced the difficult task of establishing a new nation while dealing with the growing conflict between Filipino and American forces.

Later Years and Legacy

The Philippine-American War began in 1899, and Aguinaldo led Filipino resistance against American forces. On March 23, 1901, he was captured in Palanan, Isabela. After his capture, he took an oath of allegiance to the United States and withdrew from active political leadership.

In 1935, Aguinaldo ran for president of the Philippine Commonwealth but lost to Manuel L. Quezon. He continued to take part in public activities and later became involved in matters concerning veterans and national affairs. He died on February 6, 1964, at the age of 94.

Emilio Aguinaldo remains an important figure in Philippine history because of his leadership during the revolution and his role in the establishment of the First Philippine Republic. His name is closely connected to the country's struggle for independence and the events of June 12, 1898.
