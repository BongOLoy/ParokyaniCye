Carlos Polistico Garcia

PRESIDENT FROM 1957–1961

BIOGRAPHY

Carlos Polistico Garcia was born on November 4, 1896, in Talibon, Bohol. His parents were Policronio Garcia and Ambrosia Polistico. He grew up in a family that valued education, discipline, and public service. During his childhood, Garcia developed an interest in reading, writing, and public speaking, which later helped him become a respected politician and leader.

Garcia received his early education in Bohol before continuing his studies in Cebu. He later studied at the Philippine Law School, where he prepared for a career in law and public service. He became known not only for his knowledge of law but also for his abilities as a speaker and writer. His interest in literature and poetry earned him recognition as a well-spoken and educated public figure.

Early Political Career

Before becoming president, Garcia worked as a teacher. He eventually entered politics and was elected as the representative of Bohol's third district in 1925. He served in the House of Representatives for several years before becoming governor of Bohol in 1931. His experience in local government helped him understand the needs and concerns of ordinary Filipinos.

In 1941, Garcia was elected to the Philippine Senate. However, the outbreak of World War II interrupted normal political activities. During the Japanese occupation, he supported the Filipino resistance movement. After the war, he returned to public service and continued participating in national politics.

Garcia became Vice President of the Philippines in 1953 under President Ramon Magsaysay. Aside from this position, he also served as Secretary of Foreign Affairs. In that role, he helped represent the Philippines in international discussions and strengthened the country's relationships with other nations.

Becoming President

On March 17, 1957, President Ramon Magsaysay died in an airplane crash in Cebu. As Vice President, Garcia succeeded him and became the eighth President of the Philippines. He later ran in the 1957 presidential election and won, allowing him to continue serving as the country's leader.

Garcia became president during a period when the Philippines was working to strengthen its economy and rebuild confidence in government. He continued some of the programs started by Magsaysay while introducing policies that focused on Filipino businesses, national independence, and responsible use of government resources.

Filipino First Policy

One of Garcia's most recognized contributions was the Filipino First Policy. This policy encouraged the government to give priority to Filipino citizens and locally owned businesses in important economic activities. Garcia believed that Filipino companies should have greater opportunities to grow instead of allowing foreign businesses to dominate the country's economy.

The policy also encouraged people to support Philippine-made products and local industries. Garcia believed that strengthening Filipino businesses could help the country become more economically independent. It became an important part of his administration and helped promote national pride and confidence in Filipino workers and business owners.

Government Programs and Challenges

Garcia's administration also promoted financial discipline and encouraged careful government spending. His government supported efforts to develop local industries, improve economic opportunities, and protect the country's national interests. He also continued working on international relations and cooperation with other Asian countries.

However, his administration faced several difficulties. The economy continued to experience problems, and his policies received criticism from people who believed they created difficulties for businesses and foreign investors. His government also faced concerns about corruption and the effectiveness of some economic programs.

Despite these challenges, Garcia remained associated with the idea that the Philippines should protect its economic interests and strengthen its own industries. His leadership reflected an effort to balance economic development with national independence.

Later Years and Legacy

Garcia's presidency ended in 1961 after he lost the presidential election to Diosdado Macapagal. After leaving office, he did not completely withdraw from public service. He continued to participate in national affairs and remained involved in political and constitutional work.

In 1971, Garcia became president of the Constitutional Convention, which was tasked with preparing a new constitution for the Philippines. However, he died on June 14, 1971, in Quezon City, before the convention completed its work.

Carlos P. Garcia is remembered as the president who introduced the Filipino First Policy. His administration emphasized national pride, support for Filipino-owned businesses, and the importance of developing local industries. His contributions remain part of Philippine political and economic history.
