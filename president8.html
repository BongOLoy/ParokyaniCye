Carlos Polistico Garcia

PRESIDENT FROM 1957–1961

BIOGRAPHY

Carlos Polistico Garcia was born on November 4, 1896, in Talibon, Bohol. He grew up in a family that valued education and public service. He studied in Bohol and Cebu before pursuing law at the Philippine Law School. Garcia became known for his skill in public speaking, writing, and poetry.

Before becoming president, Garcia worked as a teacher and later entered politics. He was elected representative of Bohol in 1925 and eventually became its governor. In 1941, he won a seat in the Senate. During World War II, he also supported the resistance movement against the Japanese occupation.

Garcia became Vice President in 1953 under President Ramon Magsaysay. He also served as Secretary of Foreign Affairs, helping represent the Philippines in international affairs. After Magsaysay died in a plane crash in March 1957, Garcia assumed the presidency and later won the presidential election held that same year.

Presidency and Filipino First Policy

One of Garcia's most recognized policies was the Filipino First Policy. It encouraged the government and the public to give greater support to Filipino-owned businesses, local industries, and products made in the Philippines. He believed that strengthening local businesses would help the country become more economically independent.

His administration also promoted national pride, responsible spending, and the protection of Philippine interests. However, his policies faced economic challenges and criticism. In the 1961 presidential election, Garcia lost to Diosdado Macapagal.

Later Years and Legacy

After his presidency, Garcia continued serving the country. He became president of the 1971 Constitutional Convention, which was responsible for preparing a new constitution. He passed away on June 14, 1971, in Quezon City.

Carlos P. Garcia is remembered for promoting Filipino-owned businesses and encouraging citizens to value local products. His Filipino First Policy remains one of the most recognized parts of his administration.
