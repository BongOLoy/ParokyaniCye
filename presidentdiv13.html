Jose Marcelo Ejercito

PRESIDENT • 1998 – 2001

BIOGRAPHY

Early Life and Education

Joseph Ejercito Estrada was born on April 19, 1937, in Tondo, Manila, Philippines. He came from a family with a comfortable background, but he grew up in a country where many people experienced poverty and difficult living conditions. His experiences in Manila later influenced the public image he developed as a politician.

Estrada attended Ateneo de Manila University and later studied at the Mapua Institute of Technology. However, he did not complete his college education. During his younger years, he became interested in acting and eventually entered the Philippine film industry.

Career in the Film Industry

Before entering politics, Estrada became a popular Filipino movie actor. He appeared in numerous films, often portraying characters who defended ordinary people and fought against injustice. His screen name, Joseph Estrada, became widely recognized among Filipino audiences.

His popularity in the entertainment industry helped him build a strong public following. He became known for his action films and roles that presented him as a defender of the poor. This public image later became an important part of his political campaigns.

Political Career Before the Presidency

Estrada began his political career when he was elected mayor of San Juan in 1969. During his time as mayor, he served the municipality for many years and became known for local development projects and public-service programs.

In 1987, he was elected to the Philippine Senate. He later became chairman of the Senate Committee on Cultural Communities and helped work on legislation concerning indigenous communities. In 1992, he was elected vice president under President Fidel V. Ramos.

As vice president, Estrada also served as chairman of the Presidential Anti-Crime Commission. His work against criminal activities helped strengthen his public image as a politician who promised to protect ordinary citizens.

Election and Presidency

Estrada ran for president in the 1998 national elections and won by a large margin. His campaign emphasized helping poor Filipinos, using the message of serving the ordinary people. He officially became the thirteenth President of the Philippines on June 30, 1998, succeeding Fidel V. Ramos.

His administration introduced programs intended to improve the lives of low-income families. One of its main goals was to reduce poverty and make government assistance more accessible to people who needed it.

Major Programs and Achievements

One of the best-known programs of his administration was Lingap para sa Mahirap, which aimed to provide assistance to poor communities. The program focused on helping families gain access to basic needs and government services.

Estrada also supported agricultural development and programs intended to improve the lives of farmers. His government continued infrastructure projects and worked on efforts to attract investments and strengthen the economy.

During his presidency, the government launched a military campaign against the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) in 2000. The campaign resulted in the capture of several important MILF camps, including Camp Abubakar. However, the conflict also brought serious consequences for communities in Mindanao and became a major issue during his administration.

Challenges and Impeachment

Estrada's presidency faced serious political and economic difficulties. His administration was criticized over allegations of corruption and questions about his leadership. These controversies weakened public confidence in his government.

In 2000, he faced an impeachment trial in the Senate after allegations of receiving money from illegal gambling operations and other questionable activities. The trial became a major national event. When senators voted against opening an important piece of evidence, public demonstrations grew and increased pressure on his administration.

In January 2001, large crowds gathered during the Second People Power Revolution, also known as EDSA II. The military and several government officials withdrew their support for Estrada. On January 20, 2001, he left Malacañang Palace, and Vice President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo assumed the presidency.

Later Life

After leaving office, Estrada faced legal proceedings connected to corruption allegations. In 2007, he was convicted of plunder by the Sandiganbayan but was later granted executive clemency by President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo.

Estrada eventually returned to politics. He was elected mayor of Manila in 2013 and served until 2019. His return demonstrated that he continued to have political support even after the controversies surrounding his presidency.

Legacy

Joseph Estrada remains a notable and controversial figure in Philippine history. His rise from a popular film actor to president showed the influence of public popularity in Philippine politics. His administration is remembered for programs directed toward poor communities, its military campaign in Mindanao, and the political crisis that ended his presidency.

His time in office remains an important part of the country's democratic history, particularly because of the events that led to the Second People Power Revolution in 2001.

Public leadership is measured not only by popularity, but also by responsibility and public trust.
