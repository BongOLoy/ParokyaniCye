Gloria Macaraeg Macapagal Arroyo

PRESIDENT • 2001 – 2010

BIOGRAPHY

Early Life and Education

Gloria Macapagal Arroyo was born on April 5, 1947, in San Juan, Rizal (now part of Metro Manila), Philippines. She is the daughter of former Philippine President Diosdado Macapagal and Evangelina Macaraeg. Growing up in a family involved in public service gave her early exposure to Philippine politics and government.

Arroyo received part of her early education in the Philippines before studying abroad. She attended Georgetown University in Washington, D.C., where she was a classmate of future United States President Bill Clinton. She later graduated from Assumption College in the Philippines with a degree in economics. She continued her studies at Ateneo de Manila University and earned a master's degree and a doctorate in economics from the University of the Philippines.

Academic and Political Career

Before entering national politics, Arroyo worked as an economics professor and researcher. Her academic background influenced her approach to public policy, particularly in areas involving economic growth, employment, and government spending.

She entered politics when she was elected to the Senate in 1992. During her time as a senator, she supported several laws concerning labor, education, and social development. She was reelected in 1995 and became one of the prominent members of the Senate.

In 1998, Arroyo ran for vice president and won. She served as vice president under President Joseph Estrada. During this period, she also held the position of Secretary of Social Welfare and Development, where she became involved in programs intended to assist disadvantaged families.

Becoming President

In January 2001, the Second People Power Revolution took place after public protests and growing opposition to President Joseph Estrada. Following Estrada's departure from Malacañang Palace, Arroyo assumed the presidency on January 20, 2001.

She became the fourteenth President of the Philippines. In the 2004 presidential election, she ran for a full term and was declared the winner. She continued serving as president until June 30, 2010.

Major Programs and Achievements

One of the most recognized programs introduced during Arroyo's administration was the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program, commonly known as 4Ps. Launched in 2007, it provided conditional cash assistance to qualified low-income families. The program encouraged parents to keep their children in school and attend health checkups.

Her administration also pursued economic reforms and infrastructure development. It supported improvements in roads, bridges, transportation, and other public facilities. The government also promoted investment and worked to increase the country's economic competitiveness.

Another important development during her presidency was the passage of the Expanded Value Added Tax (EVAT) law in 2005. This measure aimed to increase government revenue and help manage public debt, although it was criticized because it increased the cost of certain goods and services.

Her government also supported the development of the country's information and communications technology sector. The expansion of business process outsourcing and call center operations created employment opportunities and helped strengthen the service industry.

Challenges and Controversies

Arroyo's presidency faced several controversies and political challenges. Her administration was criticized over allegations of corruption, election irregularities, and questions about government accountability.

In 2005, recordings connected to allegations of interference in the 2004 presidential election became public. The controversy became widely known as the "Hello Garci" scandal. It caused public criticism and calls for her resignation, although she remained in office.

Her administration also faced security challenges, including armed conflicts and political unrest in different parts of the country. These issues affected public confidence and remained difficult problems throughout her presidency.

End of Presidency and Later Political Career

Arroyo completed her presidential term on June 30, 2010. She was succeeded by Benigno Aquino III. After leaving the presidency, she was elected as the representative of the Second District of Pampanga in the House of Representatives.

She later became Speaker of the House of Representatives in 2018, making her one of the few former Philippine presidents to hold another major government position after leaving Malacañang. She continued to participate in Philippine politics and public service.

Legacy

Gloria Macapagal Arroyo is remembered for her long presidency, economic policies, infrastructure projects, and the introduction of the 4Ps program. Her administration also faced major controversies that influenced public discussions about elections, corruption, and political accountability.

Her time as president remains an important period in modern Philippine history because of its economic developments, social programs, and political challenges.
