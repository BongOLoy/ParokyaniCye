Maria Corazon Cojuangco Aquino

PRESIDENT • 1986 – 1992

BIOGRAPHY

Maria Corazon Sumulong Cojuangco Aquino was born on January 25, 1933, in Paniqui, Tarlac, Philippines. She came from the well-known Cojuangco family. Her father, Jose Cojuangco, was a businessman and politician, while her mother, Demetria Sumulong, came from a family involved in public service. Aquino grew up in a family that valued education and community involvement.

During her childhood, Aquino studied in several schools in the Philippines and later continued her education in the United States. She attended schools in New York and studied at the College of Notre Dame in Maryland before completing further studies at the College of Mount Saint Vincent in New York. Her education helped develop her interest in public affairs and social issues.

Family Life and Political Background

In 1954, Corazon Aquino married Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr., who later became a prominent opposition politician. They had five children: Maria Elena, Aurora Corazon, Benigno III, Victoria Elisa, and Kristina Bernadette. For many years, Aquino focused mainly on raising her family while her husband pursued a career in politics.

Her husband became a well-known critic of President Ferdinand Marcos Sr. During the declaration of Martial Law in 1972, Ninoy Aquino was arrested and imprisoned. He spent several years in detention before being allowed to travel to the United States for medical treatment. Corazon remained supportive of her husband and became increasingly involved in activities opposing authoritarian rule.

On August 21, 1983, Ninoy Aquino was assassinated shortly after returning to the Philippines. His death caused widespread public anger and led to large demonstrations against the Marcos administration. Corazon Aquino became one of the most recognized figures of the opposition movement and gained support from Filipinos calling for democratic change.

Presidential Election of 1986

In 1985, Ferdinand Marcos announced a snap presidential election to be held in February 1986. Corazon Aquino became the opposition's presidential candidate, with Salvador Laurel as her running mate. Her campaign called for honest government, political freedom, and the restoration of democracy.

The election was marked by allegations of fraud, irregularities, and disputes over the results. Both Marcos and Aquino's supporters claimed victory. The controversy increased public opposition to the government and contributed to a major political crisis.

EDSA People Power Revolution

From February 22 to 25, 1986, millions of Filipinos participated in demonstrations along Epifanio de los Santos Avenue, commonly known as EDSA. The People Power Revolution brought together citizens, religious groups, military members, and political supporters who wanted an end to Marcos's rule.

The demonstrations were largely peaceful and became a powerful symbol of unity and the demand for democratic government. Ferdinand Marcos and his family left the country on February 25, 1986. On the same day, Corazon Aquino took her oath as president, becoming the first woman to serve as President of the Philippines.

Presidency and Restoration of Democracy

When Aquino became president, she inherited a country facing serious political, economic, and social problems. Her administration focused on restoring democratic institutions, rebuilding public trust, and addressing the effects of years of authoritarian rule.

One of her major accomplishments was the restoration of democratic government. In 1986, a new constitutional commission was formed to prepare a constitution. The proposed 1987 Constitution was approved by Filipino voters in a national plebiscite on February 2, 1987. It restored important democratic principles, protected civil liberties, and established limits on presidential power.

Her administration also restored the country's Congress and held elections for national and local positions. These changes helped rebuild democratic processes and allowed citizens to participate more actively in choosing their leaders.

Land Reform and Economic Challenges

Aquino's government also attempted to address poverty and inequality in rural communities. In 1988, she signed Republic Act No. 6657, known as the Comprehensive Agrarian Reform Law. This program aimed to distribute agricultural land to qualified farmers and provide support for agricultural development.

However, implementing the land reform program proved difficult. Problems involving land ownership, funding, and disagreements among landowners and farmers slowed its progress. The administration also faced economic difficulties, including government debt, unemployment, and the challenge of rebuilding investor confidence.

Despite these problems, the government worked to improve the country's international relationships and encourage economic recovery. Aquino's administration also faced several coup attempts by military groups, creating continuing challenges to political stability.

National Challenges and Important Events

During Aquino's presidency, the Philippines experienced several major natural disasters. The 1990 Luzon earthquake caused widespread destruction and loss of life. In 1991, the eruption of Mount Pinatubo affected many communities, especially in Central Luzon, and forced thousands of families to leave their homes.

Another important event occurred in 1991 when the Philippine Senate rejected a proposed treaty that would have allowed United States military bases to remain in the country. This decision marked a major change in Philippine foreign relations and national policy.

End of Presidency and Later Life

Corazon Aquino served as president until June 30, 1992. She chose not to seek another term, following the six-year presidential limit under the 1987 Constitution. She was succeeded by Fidel V. Ramos, who won the 1992 presidential election.

After leaving office, Aquino continued to participate in public discussions and activities supporting democracy and good governance. She remained a respected public figure and sometimes spoke about national issues, political leadership, and the importance of protecting democratic freedoms.

Corazon Aquino died on August 1, 2009, in Makati City, after battling colorectal cancer. Her death led to widespread mourning across the country. Many Filipinos remembered her role in the People Power Revolution and the return of democratic government.

Legacy

Corazon C. Aquino is remembered as the first female President of the Philippines and a central figure in the restoration of democracy after the Marcos dictatorship. Her leadership helped bring back constitutional government and encouraged greater public participation in national affairs.

Although her administration faced economic difficulties, political instability, and criticism over the implementation of its programs, her presidency remains an important chapter in Philippine history. The EDSA People Power Revolution continues to be remembered as a significant example of citizens coming together to demand political change.

Remembered for leading the return of democracy and becoming the first female President of the Philippines.
