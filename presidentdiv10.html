Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos

PRESIDENT • 1965 – 1986

BIOGRAPHY

Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos was born on September 11, 1917, in Sarrat, Ilocos Norte, Philippines. His parents were Mariano Marcos and Josefa Edralin. He studied in several schools before attending the University of the Philippines, where he studied law. He passed the Bar Examination in 1939 and later became a lawyer.

Marcos entered politics after World War II. He was elected to the House of Representatives in 1949 and served as a congressman for several years. He later became a senator in 1959 and eventually served as Senate President from 1963 to 1965. His political career helped him gain national recognition and prepare for the presidency.

In 1954, Marcos married Imelda Romualdez. He ran for president in 1965 and won the election, defeating incumbent President Diosdado Macapagal. He became the tenth president of the Philippines and was reelected in 1969, becoming the first Philippine president to win a second full term.

Presidency and Martial Law

During the early years of his presidency, Marcos promoted infrastructure projects, including roads, bridges, schools, and public buildings. His administration also introduced programs involving agriculture, transportation, and national development. However, his government faced growing political opposition, economic difficulties, and social unrest.

On September 21, 1972, Marcos placed the Philippines under Martial Law through Proclamation No. 1081. His government restricted civil liberties, controlled media organizations, and arrested political opponents and critics. The period was also marked by reports of human rights abuses, censorship, and the concentration of power in the presidency.

Martial Law was formally lifted in 1981, but Marcos continued to govern under strong executive authority. In the years that followed, the country experienced serious economic problems and increasing public opposition. The assassination of opposition leader Benigno Aquino Jr. in 1983 further increased criticism of the government.

End of Presidency and Legacy

In February 1986, a snap presidential election was held amid widespread controversy. Protests and public demonstrations followed, leading to the People Power Revolution along Epifanio de los Santos Avenue, commonly known as EDSA. After four days of demonstrations, Marcos left the Philippines with his family on February 25, 1986, ending his presidency.

Ferdinand Marcos Sr. died on September 28, 1989, in Honolulu, Hawaii. His time as president remains one of the most debated periods in Philippine history. His administration is remembered for its infrastructure projects, the declaration of Martial Law, and the serious political, economic, and human rights issues that affected the country.
