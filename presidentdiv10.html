Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos

PRESIDENT • 1965 – 1986

BIOGRAPHY

Ferdinand Emmanuel Edralin Marcos was born on September 11, 1917, in Sarrat, Ilocos Norte, Philippines. He was the son of Mariano Marcos, a lawyer and politician, and Josefa Edralin, a teacher. He grew up in a family that valued education and public service. During his early years, Marcos attended schools in Ilocos Norte before continuing his studies in Manila.

Marcos studied law at the University of the Philippines. In 1939, he passed the Philippine Bar Examination and became a lawyer. His early career was also affected by a murder case involving the death of Julio Nalundasan, a political opponent of his father. Marcos was convicted in the case but was later acquitted by the Supreme Court after an appeal.

Early Political Career

During World War II, Marcos served in the Philippine Army and was involved in military activities during the Japanese occupation. After the war, he entered national politics. In 1949, he was elected as a representative of Ilocos Norte in the House of Representatives. He served three terms in Congress and became known as an active member of the political community.

In 1959, Marcos was elected to the Senate. He later became Senate President in 1963, a position that increased his influence in national government. His political experience and growing popularity helped prepare him for a presidential campaign.

In 1954, Marcos married Imelda Romualdez. In the 1965 presidential election, he ran against incumbent President Diosdado Macapagal and won. He officially became the tenth President of the Philippines on December 30, 1965.

First Term and National Development

During his first years as president, Marcos promoted infrastructure development and government construction projects. His administration supported the building and improvement of roads, bridges, schools, hospitals, and public facilities. These projects were presented as part of his efforts to modernize the country and improve transportation and public services.

His government also introduced programs related to agriculture, irrigation, and rural development. Some infrastructure projects helped connect communities and improve access to important services. However, these developments also involved large government expenditures, and the country's growing debt became a major concern in later years.

In 1969, Marcos won reelection and became the first Philippine president to serve two full elected terms. His second term began during a period of increasing political tension, economic problems, and public demonstrations.

Martial Law and Authoritarian Rule

During the early 1970s, the Philippines experienced growing unrest, political conflict, and security concerns. On September 21, 1972, Marcos signed Proclamation No. 1081, placing the country under Martial Law. The declaration was publicly announced on September 23, 1972.

Under Martial Law, the government gained greater control over the country. Congress was shut down, media organizations were closed or placed under government control, and many political opponents, activists, journalists, and other critics were arrested. Civil liberties and democratic institutions were severely restricted.

This period was marked by widespread reports of human rights violations, including arbitrary detention, torture, censorship, and abuses by government forces. Many Filipinos experienced fear and restrictions on their freedom. The concentration of political power also weakened democratic checks and balances.

In 1973, a new constitution was introduced, changing the structure of the Philippine government. Although Martial Law was formally lifted in January 1981, Marcos continued to exercise strong executive authority. His government remained controversial because of restrictions on political freedoms and continuing reports of human rights abuses.

Economic Problems and Growing Opposition

During the later years of Marcos's rule, the Philippines experienced serious economic difficulties. Government debt increased, poverty remained a major problem, and economic opportunities became difficult for many Filipinos. Corruption and favoritism toward people connected to the administration also became major sources of criticism.

On August 21, 1983, opposition leader Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr. was assassinated shortly after returning to the Philippines. His death caused widespread public outrage and strengthened opposition to the Marcos administration. It also became an important turning point in the country's political history.

EDSA People Power Revolution

In February 1986, Marcos called for a snap presidential election. He ran against Corazon C. Aquino, the widow of Benigno Aquino Jr. The election was surrounded by allegations of fraud and irregularities. Both sides claimed victory, leading to a serious political crisis.

Following the election, large numbers of Filipinos gathered along Epifanio de los Santos Avenue, commonly known as EDSA. The demonstrations became known as the People Power Revolution. Millions of citizens participated in peaceful protests calling for political change and the end of Marcos's rule.

After several days of demonstrations, Marcos and his family left the Philippines on February 25, 1986, and went into exile in Hawaii. His departure ended his twenty-year presidency and marked a major turning point in Philippine history. Corazon Aquino became president and led the restoration of democratic institutions.

Later Life and Legacy

Marcos lived in Honolulu, Hawaii, after leaving the Philippines. He died on September 28, 1989, at the age of 72. His remains were kept in Hawaii for several years before being returned to the Philippines.

Ferdinand Marcos Sr. remains one of the most controversial figures in Philippine history. His administration is associated with major infrastructure projects and changes in government, but also with authoritarian rule, human rights violations, corruption, and serious economic difficulties.

His presidency is especially remembered for the declaration of Martial Law and the events that led to the 1986 EDSA People Power Revolution. This period continues to be studied as an important lesson about democracy, government accountability, and the protection of human rights.

His presidency remains a significant and controversial chapter in the history of the Philippines.
