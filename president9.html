Diosdado Pangan Macapagal

PRESIDENT • 1961 – 1965

BIOGRAPHY

Diosdado Pangan Macapagal was born on September 28, 1910, in Lubao, Pampanga, Philippines. He came from a poor farming family and experienced financial difficulties during his childhood. His parents were Urbano Macapagal and Romana Pangan. Growing up in a simple household helped him understand the struggles of ordinary Filipinos, especially those living in rural communities.

Despite his family's limited resources, Macapagal worked hard to continue his education. He studied at the University of the Philippines and later at the University of Santo Tomas, where he completed his law studies. He became a lawyer and performed exceptionally well in the 1936 Bar Examination, ranking third. His education and determination helped him build a career in law and public service.

Early Career and Political Beginnings

Before becoming a national leader, Macapagal worked as a lawyer and served in government. During World War II, he became involved in activities connected with the Philippine government-in-exile. After the war, he continued his legal career and entered electoral politics.

In 1949, Macapagal was elected representative of Pampanga's first district in the House of Representatives. He served for several years and became known for his speeches and participation in legislative work. In 1957, he was elected Vice President under President Carlos P. Garcia. He also served as president of the Liberal Party, gaining more experience in national politics.

In the 1961 presidential election, Macapagal ran against incumbent President Garcia. He won the election and became the ninth President of the Philippines. His campaign focused on honest government, economic improvement, and helping ordinary Filipinos.

Presidency and Economic Reforms

Macapagal assumed office on December 30, 1961. His administration aimed to strengthen the Philippine economy and address poverty. He supported policies intended to encourage economic growth, improve government services, and provide better opportunities for farmers and workers.

One of his major economic decisions was the removal of certain government controls on foreign exchange. His administration hoped that allowing the value of the peso to be determined more freely would encourage investment and improve the country's economic position. However, this change also brought difficulties, including higher prices for some imported goods.

Agricultural Land Reform

One of Macapagal's most important achievements was the signing of the Agricultural Land Reform Code in 1963, also known as Republic Act No. 3844. This law aimed to replace the traditional share-tenancy system with a system that gave farmers greater security and opportunities to own the land they cultivated.

The reform was intended to improve the living conditions of farmers, reduce unfair treatment in agricultural communities, and encourage greater productivity. It represented an important effort to address long-standing problems in Philippine agriculture. However, implementing land reform was difficult because of financial limitations and resistance to changes in land ownership and tenancy.

Independence Day and National Identity

Another important decision during Macapagal's presidency was changing the official celebration of Philippine Independence Day. In 1962, he issued a proclamation recognizing June 12 as Independence Day, replacing the previous official date of July 4. The change honored the declaration of independence made by Emilio Aguinaldo in Kawit, Cavite, on June 12, 1898.

In 1964, Republic Act No. 4166 formally established June 12 as Philippine Independence Day. This decision helped strengthen the country's recognition of its 1898 declaration of independence and its struggle for freedom.

Challenges During His Administration

Although Macapagal introduced several reforms, his administration faced economic and political challenges. Rising prices and difficulties affecting some sectors of the economy caused concern among citizens. Some of his programs also faced opposition, making it difficult to achieve all the goals of his administration.

His government also dealt with international issues, including the Philippines' claim over Sabah and its relationships with neighboring countries. These matters became important parts of Philippine foreign policy during his presidency.

In the 1965 presidential election, Macapagal ran for another term but was defeated by Ferdinand Marcos. His presidency ended on December 30, 1965, after four years in office.

Later Life and Legacy

After leaving the presidency, Macapagal continued to participate in public affairs. In 1971, he became president of the Constitutional Convention, which was responsible for preparing a new constitution for the country. He continued to take part in discussions about the nation's political future and constitutional government.

Macapagal later remained active in public service and national discussions. He also became known as the father of former Philippine President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo. He died on April 21, 1997, in Makati City, at the age of 86.

Diosdado Macapagal is remembered for his efforts to improve the lives of farmers, introduce economic reforms, and recognize June 12 as the country's Independence Day. His leadership remains an important part of Philippine history, particularly in discussions about land reform and national identity.
