Manuel Acuña Roxas

PRESIDENT • 1946 – 1948

BIOGRAPHY

Manuel Acuña Roxas was born on January 1, 1892, in Capiz, Philippines. He became the fifth president of the Philippines and the first president of the independent Third Philippine Republic. He studied law at the University of the Philippines and graduated in 1913. He was also recognized for his excellent performance in the country's first Bar examinations.

Roxas began his political career in Capiz, where he served in local government before becoming governor. He was later elected to the Philippine House of Representatives and eventually became its Speaker. He also worked with Sergio Osmeña in the Os-Rox Mission, which sought to advance Philippine independence from the United States.

During the Commonwealth period, Roxas held important government positions, including Secretary of Finance. He also participated in the preparations for Philippine independence and became one of the country's leading political figures.

Presidency and Philippine Independence

Roxas won the 1946 presidential election. He became the last president of the Commonwealth of the Philippines on May 28, 1946. On July 4, 1946, the United States officially recognized Philippine independence, and Roxas became the first president of the independent Third Republic.

His administration focused on rebuilding the country after World War II. Many communities, roads, buildings, and public services had been damaged during the war. His government worked on economic recovery and rebuilding the nation's institutions. However, his presidency also faced challenges involving poverty, economic policies, and disagreements over the country's direction after independence.

Later Life and Legacy

Roxas served as president until his death on April 15, 1948. He died of a heart attack while speaking at Clark Air Base in Pampanga. Vice President Elpidio Quirino succeeded him as president.

Manuel Roxas is remembered as the leader who guided the Philippines during its transition from the Commonwealth government to an independent republic after World War II.
