Rodrigo Roa Duterte

PRESIDENT • 2016 – 2022

BIOGRAPHY

Early Life and Family Background

Rodrigo Roa Duterte was born on March 28, 1945, in Maasin, Leyte, Philippines. He was the son of Vicente Duterte, who later became governor of Davao, and Soledad Roa, a teacher and community leader. His family eventually settled in Davao, where he spent much of his childhood and early life.

Duterte studied at the Lyceum of the Philippines University, where he earned a degree in Political Science. He later studied law at San Beda College of Law and passed the Philippine Bar Examination in 1973. His legal education helped prepare him for his future work in public service.

Early Career and Political Beginnings

Before becoming a politician, Duterte worked as a lawyer and served as a government prosecutor in Davao City. In 1986, following the People Power Revolution, he became a vice mayor of Davao City. He was later elected mayor in 1988.

Duterte served several terms as mayor of Davao City, becoming known for his strong leadership style and strict approach to law enforcement. During his years in local government, the city experienced changes in public safety, infrastructure, and local services. His leadership also attracted criticism from human rights groups over allegations of abuses and extrajudicial killings.

His political career included periods as mayor, vice mayor, and representative of Davao City's First District in the House of Representatives. His long experience in local government helped him build a strong political following, especially in Mindanao.

Election and Presidency

In 2016, Duterte ran for president of the Philippines. His campaign focused on fighting illegal drugs and crime, improving public order, and addressing problems outside Metro Manila. He won the presidential election and became the sixteenth President of the Philippines on June 30, 2016.

His administration promoted a strong law-and-order approach and promised to improve government services and infrastructure. He also emphasized the development of provinces and cities outside the capital region.

War on Drugs and Public Safety

One of the most recognized and controversial policies of Duterte's presidency was his campaign against illegal drugs, commonly called the "War on Drugs." The government carried out police operations throughout the country, leading to thousands of deaths.

The campaign received support from people who believed it would help reduce crime and illegal drug activity. However, it also faced serious criticism from human rights organizations, local groups, and international institutions because of allegations of unlawful killings and abuses. These concerns became a major issue in discussions about his administration.

Infrastructure and Economic Programs

Duterte introduced the "Build, Build, Build" program, which aimed to improve the country's infrastructure. It included projects involving roads, bridges, airports, seaports, and public transportation. The program was intended to improve travel, support businesses, and connect communities across the Philippines.

His administration also signed the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion, or TRAIN Law, in 2017. The law changed the country's tax system, including reducing personal income taxes for many workers while increasing taxes on certain goods and services.

Another important measure was the Universal Health Care Act, signed in 2019. It aimed to make healthcare services more accessible to Filipinos by expanding health insurance coverage and strengthening the country's healthcare system.

Peace and Regional Development

During Duterte's presidency, the Bangsamoro Organic Law was signed in 2018. It created the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao, replacing the Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao. The law was an important step in the peace process and aimed to provide greater self-governance to the region.

His administration also continued efforts to address long-standing conflicts in Mindanao. These efforts involved cooperation with local leaders and groups to support peace, development, and stability in the region.

Challenges and Controversies

Duterte's administration faced several major challenges. In 2017, armed groups occupied parts of Marawi City, leading to a months-long conflict that caused destruction and forced many residents to leave their homes. The government declared martial law across Mindanao during the crisis.

Another major challenge was the COVID-19 pandemic, which reached the Philippines in 2020. The government introduced community quarantine measures, travel restrictions, and public health rules to control the spread of the virus. These measures affected schools, businesses, workers, and everyday life throughout the country.

His administration also faced criticism over its handling of the pandemic, human rights concerns, press freedom issues, and government spending. These controversies became important parts of public debate during his presidency.

End of Presidency and Later Political Career

Duterte completed his six-year presidential term on June 30, 2022. He was succeeded by Ferdinand "Bongbong" Marcos Jr. After leaving the presidency, Duterte returned to Davao City and continued to take part in Philippine politics.

In 2025, Duterte was arrested under a warrant issued by the International Criminal Court in connection with its investigation into alleged crimes against humanity related to the campaign against illegal drugs. The case became another major development connected to his presidency.

Legacy

Rodrigo Duterte remains one of the most debated presidents in modern Philippine history. Supporters point to his infrastructure projects, regional development efforts, and strong leadership style. Critics focus on human rights concerns, the drug campaign, and issues involving democratic institutions.

His presidency had a lasting effect on Philippine politics and society. His administration's policies, achievements, and controversies continue to influence discussions about leadership, public safety, human rights, and the responsibilities of government.

A nation's leadership is remembered through its decisions and the lasting effects they have on its people.
